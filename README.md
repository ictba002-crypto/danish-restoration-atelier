# Danish Revival

Danish Modern L.A. Redesign

Homepage + Product Detail Page

1. Project Context (for the designer / AI tool)

Danish Modern L.A. sources authentic vintage Danish & Mid-Century Modern furniture, restores it in-house with skilled artisans, and resells it. Because each piece is hand-refurbished by real craftspeople using period-correct techniques, prices sit above mass furniture retailers. The current site doesn't communicate that story — it reads like a generic e-commerce catalog instead of a heritage atelier.

Goal: Redesign the homepage and product detail page (PDP) so a first-time visitor immediately feels: this is rare, authentic, expertly restored, and worth the price. Keep the existing logo unchanged; everything else should elevate around it.

2. Design Brief / Prompt (paste into Figma / Figma AI / share with designer)

Design a premium, editorial-style homepage and product detail page for Danish Modern L.A., a Los Angeles gallery that sources, restores, and sells authentic vintage Danish and Mid-Century Modern furniture (est. 2009). The brand's value isn't "furniture for sale" — it's curated, restored design history. The UI should feel closer to a design gallery, auction house, or high-end editorial magazine than a typical furniture e-commerce store.

Brand Feel / Art Direction

Tone: Warm, editorial, confident, understated luxury — think Kinfolk magazine × 1stDibs × a boutique LA gallery.

Avoid: Bright "sale banner" retail energy, cluttered grids, stock-photo gloss, busy iconography.

Emphasize: Negative space, large photography, provenance storytelling, craftsmanship.

Color Palette

Base: warm off-white / bone (#F7F3EC), walnut brown (#4A362A), charcoal ink (#2B2621)

Accent: muted brass / ochre (#B8895B) for CTAs and highlights

Optional secondary: deep forest or teak green (#3F4A3A) as a rare accent, echoing mid-century upholstery tones

No pure black or pure white — keep everything warm-toned to feel "aged paper / gallery wall"

Typography

Headlines: A refined serif or high-contrast display serif (e.g., feel of Canela, GT Sectra, or Times-adjacent) — evokes heritage and craftsmanship

Body/UI: A clean grotesk sans (e.g., feel of Neue Haas, Inter, or Söhne) for legibility and modern contrast against the serif

Generous line-height and letter-spacing on all-caps labels (category tags, nav) to feel curated, not cramped

Imagery Direction

Product photography: soft natural light, neutral backgrounds, occasional in-situ "styled room" shots — never harsh studio white-box lighting

Include close-up detail shots (wood grain, joinery, original hardware, reupholstered fabric texture) to visually prove craftsmanship

Before/after restoration imagery where possible — this is a strong trust/value signal unique to this brand

3. Homepage — Section-by-Section Layout

Header

Existing logo, centered or left-aligned

Minimal nav: New Arrivals / Furniture / Fine Art / Lighting / Services / About / Contact

Thin utility bar above nav: "Est. 2009 · Los Angeles" + trust marks (Authenticity Guaranteed · 5.0★ 549 Reviews)

Hero

Full-bleed lifestyle photo of a restored piece in a styled room

Overlay headline (serif): e.g. "Vintage Danish Design, Restored by Hand"

Subline (sans): one line on sourcing + restoration promise

Single CTA: "Explore the Collection"

Brand Story Strip (new section — currently missing)

Short 2–3 sentence narrative: sourced from Denmark & Scandinavia → restored in our LA studio by master craftsmen → one-of-a-kind, not reproductions

Small "Est. 2009 · 15+ Years of Restoration Craft" badge/stat block

Optional: 3 icons/stats — Years in Business, Pieces Restored, Designers Represented

Shop by Category

Large editorial tiles (2–4 per row) instead of small icon grid — Furniture / Lighting / Fine Art / Seating etc., each with full-bleed photography and serif label overlay

Featured Restoration / "Craft in Progress" (new section)

Before/after slider or side-by-side imagery of a restoration

Short copy on the artisan process (materials, techniques, time invested)

This directly justifies price positioning

New Arrivals Carousel

Horizontal scroll, generous image size, minimal card chrome (image, designer name, piece name, price)

Shop by Designer

Editorial cards for Hans Wegner, Finn Juhl, Milo Baughman, Børge Mogensen, etc. — portrait + 1-line bio teaser, links to filtered collection

Trust Section

Authenticity Guaranteed / Worldwide Shipping / Returns / Pro Customer Service — redesigned as elegant iconography + serif micro-headlines, not generic e-comm badge icons

Reviews block: 5.0/5 · 549 Reviews, pull 2–3 short testimonials

Video / Gallery Tour

Existing gallery video, presented full-width with subtle framing (feels like a gallery walkthrough, not a marketing reel)

Newsletter

Simple, elegant signup band: "Get first access to new restorations"

Footer

Reorganized: Shop / Company (Story, Press, Reviews) / Services (Refinishing & Upholstery, Trade Program, We Buy, Affirm) / Contact + social

4. Product Detail Page (PDP) Layout

Breadcrumb (Home / Category / Designer / Product)

Gallery

Large primary image, thumbnail rail below/side

Include at least one "detail" close-up shot and, where applicable, a before/after or restoration-process image

Product Info Panel

Designer name (linked) + Product name (serif)

Era / Year of design + Origin (e.g., "Denmark, c. 1960s")

Condition & Restoration note: what was restored, materials used, artisan process — this is the key trust/value section, give it real visual weight, not a small paragraph buried below

Dimensions, materials

Price + Affirm financing line

CTAs: Add to Cart / Inquire / Request a Hold

Provenance & Authenticity Block

Short badge/callout: "Authenticated by our Mid-Century Modern experts" + brand's years of experience

Designer Spotlight

Mini bio card of the designer (reuse homepage designer-card style) to add context/value

You May Also Like

Related pieces, same editorial card style as homepage
